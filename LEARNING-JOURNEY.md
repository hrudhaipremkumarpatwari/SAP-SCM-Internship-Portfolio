# SAP SCM Learning Journey

## From Network Design to Continuous Improvement

The six assessments are organized as one connected supply-chain story.

### 1. Network Planning — Week 1

The first assessment establishes the supply-chain structure: suppliers, manufacturing, distribution locations, transportation lanes, sourcing relationships and planning scope. The key learning is that a supply plan depends on the physical and logical network represented in planning master data.

### 2. Master Data & Inventory — Week 2

The second assessment moves from network structure to planning-data quality. Products, locations, resources, lead times, lot sizes, safety stock and sourcing parameters are reviewed because incorrect master data can produce poor planning results even when demand is correct.

### 3. Logistics & Distribution — Week 3

The third assessment focuses on moving material through the network. Distribution allocations, inventory transfers, routes and shipment consolidation are evaluated to connect supply planning with transportation and service outcomes.

### 4. Production Planning & Scheduling — Week 4

The fourth assessment brings demand into the plant. Finite-capacity scheduling, changeovers, resource constraints and bottleneck recovery are used to show why a mathematically sufficient demand plan may still be operationally fragile.

### 5. Integration & Collaboration — Week 5

The fifth assessment connects the functions. A supplier commitment change is followed through procurement, planning, production, inventory, warehouse, transportation and customer-facing decisions. The focus is on reliable data exchange, ownership, reconciliation and exception handling.

### 6. Performance & Continuous Improvement — Week 6

The final assessment closes the loop. Simulated KPI trends are reviewed, gaps are identified, root-cause hypotheses are formed, controls are proposed and a 30/60/90-day improvement roadmap is developed using PDCA thinking.

## Overall Model

```text
PLAN THE NETWORK
      ↓
CONTROL THE DATA
      ↓
MOVE THE INVENTORY
      ↓
SCHEDULE THE PLANT
      ↓
CONNECT THE FUNCTIONS
      ↓
MEASURE + IMPROVE
```

The overall learning objective is to understand SAP SCM not as isolated transactions, but as a connected planning and decision-support process where master data, constraints, integration events and KPIs influence one another.
