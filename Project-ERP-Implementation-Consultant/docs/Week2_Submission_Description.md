# Week 2 Submission Description

This submission develops a configuration and customization blueprint for a hypothetical ERP implementation at Horizon Foods Ltd., the same fictional packaged-food manufacturer introduced in Week 1. It focuses on two connected functional areas: purchasing and inventory processes aligned to common SAP Sourcing and Procurement/MM concepts, and production planning aligned to PP concepts. The document makes clear that this is a design exercise: no SAP or other ERP system was accessed or configured, and no live-system screenshot or implementation result is claimed.

The blueprint sets out proposed master-data ownership, purchase approval routing, purchase-order-referenced goods receipt, an illustrative exception tolerance, unit-of-measure governance, plant-level planning inputs, approved bills of material and routings, and a planner review before production-order release. It explains why each decision matters and identifies the business owner and acceptance evidence needed before product-specific configuration. A flow diagram shows the sequence from fit-to-standard review through design, role and language decisions, testing, and approval. A separate spreadsheet-ready decision register makes the configuration assumptions easy to sort and adapt. An eight-case rule validation register and static configuration mock-up provide auditable illustrative evidence; neither represents a software test or live screenshot.

The language section pairs stable English technical labels with simplified Chinese HSK 4 learning cues such as purchase order, quantity, plan, confirm, standard, and quality, with pinyin and short task instructions. It recommends bilingual review for critical wording rather than relying on machine translation or replacing exact ERP field identifiers. The report also includes testable acceptance conditions, implementation risks, mitigations, references to SAP Learning material on purchasing and MRP, and a reflection on why decision ownership and validation should precede system changes. All company characteristics, materials, quantities, currency thresholds, process rules, and expected outcomes are illustrative training assumptions. They should be replaced with approved requirements and verified in an authorized system before any real implementation.
