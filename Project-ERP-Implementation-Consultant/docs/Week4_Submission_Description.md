# Week 4 Submission Description

This submission combines a hypothetical post-implementation review and a role-based end-user training plan for the fictional Horizon Foods ERP case. The report explicitly labels every performance figure, denominator, attendance count, adoption result and assessment outcome as simulated. It does not claim a real ERP go-live, production telemetry, learner assessment or realized benefit. The review compares illustrative legacy/early-use baselines with day-90 sample results and supplies numerators and denominators so each KPI can be recalculated.

The scorecard covers inventory record accuracy, production schedule adherence, purchase-order approval cycle time, order-entry error rate, active user adoption and training task pass rate. The document explains percentage-point change, relative cycle-time reduction and the limits of small samples. It cautions that the patterns do not prove ERP causation and calls for consistent definitions, equivalent periods and source validation before any real reporting. A continuous-improvement table turns each finding into an owner-led action with a review trigger, including cycle-count follow-up, schedule exception analysis, approval-wait breakdown, targeted job aids, and make-up coaching. A spreadsheet-ready scorecard preserves the sample inputs and calculation formulas. A dashboard visual compares four percentage KPIs and calls out the separate approval-time and training-pass calculations, all marked as simulated.

The training plan covers buyers and approvers, warehouse/receiving users, planners and supervisors, and finance/support roles. Sessions use practical tasks tied to the Week 2 procurement/production blueprint and Week 3 sample migration scenario. It defines learning objectives, durations, exercise evidence, attendance and reassessment rules. In the invented scenario, 27 of 30 users attend and 24 of 27 assessed learners pass; absences and unsuccessful checks remain open until make-up and reassessment. Mandarin HSK 4 cues are integrated into concise job instructions with pinyin, while exact English ERP field identifiers remain visible and critical translations require bilingual review. The report closes with lessons, measurement limitations and public SAP learning references. All figures and assumptions are for portfolio practice only.
