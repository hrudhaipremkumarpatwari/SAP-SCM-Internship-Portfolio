# SAP SCM Internship Portfolio

**Hrudhai Prem Kumar Patwari | SAP SCM / SAP MM Learning Portfolio**

This repository is the central index for my six-assessment SAP SCM internship series. The assessments are linked as a progressive learning journey covering supply-chain network planning, master data and inventory optimization, logistics and distribution, production planning and scheduling, supply-chain integration and collaboration, and performance evaluation with continuous improvement.

> **Portfolio scope:** The projects are internship/academic simulations and configuration blueprints. Company details, operational quantities, costs, capacities, KPIs and scenario results are illustrative unless a source is explicitly identified. The repositories do not claim live SAP production execution.

## Learning Progression

```text
Week 1
Supply Chain Network Planning
        ↓
Week 2

        ↓
Week 3
Logistics + Distribution Network Design
        ↓
Week 4
Production Planning + Scheduling
        ↓

        ↓
```

The sequence moves from designing the supply network, to making planning data reliable, to moving inventory efficiently, to creating feasible production schedules, to connecting the functions and managing exceptions, and finally to measuring performance and improving the process.

## How to Review the Portfolio

1. Start with Week 1 to understand the modeled supply-chain network.
2. Continue to Week 2 to see how master-data quality and inventory parameters affect planning.
3. Review Week 3 for distribution and transportation decisions.
4. Review Week 4 for capacity-aware production scheduling and bottleneck recovery.
5. Review Week 5 for integration, collaboration and exception-management logic.
6. Finish with Week 6 for KPI-based performance evaluation and continuous improvement.
7. Open each final Word report for the detailed assessment narrative and worked examples.

## Important Simulation Note

The portfolio is designed to demonstrate reasoning, SAP functional understanding, configuration thinking, calculations, validation and documentation. Where a report describes a SAP transaction, configuration sequence, planning run, integration check or dashboard, it should be read as a practical learning/configuration blueprint unless the report explicitly identifies a real executed system result.
